ICT 401 Seminar Project

I was President and Organizing Lead of an 18-student team that planned and delivered a live academic webinar, “Distraction or Innovation? Student Life in a Hyperconnected World,” on 31 July 2025.

My role
President / Organizing Lead (Organizing secretary), leading the 5-person Organizing team
Team
18 students in four teams: Organizing (5), Content/Convenor (4), Chair/Moderator (5), Technical/Documentation (4)
Format
Live webinar on Google Meet, with a course lecturer as advisor
Delivered
4 student research presentations with Q&A, in about 90 minutes
Audience
ICT students from seminar and research methodology classes, faculty, and ICT professionals
Result
60 survey respondents gave a mean satisfaction score of 4.29 out of 5 (85.9%)

Context

ICT 401 (Seminar in ICT) asks each team to plan and run a seminar. Ours looked at how technology affects students’ learning, focus, and well-being. Four student research groups presented: media multitasking and academic performance, social media and mental health, doomscrolling, and how Discord supports active learning.

The plan

We worked to an 8-week plan built on PDCA (plan, do, check, act), with SMART goals for each meeting:

  • Weeks 1 to 2: choose the topic, assign responsibilities, and finish the project proposal.
  • Weeks 3 to 4: select the research topics to present and draft the agenda.
  • Weeks 5 to 6: meet the researchers and organizing teams, then rehearse.
  • Week 7: hold the seminar and collect the survey.
  • Week 8: evaluate the feedback and submit the final report.

A public call for papers fixed the dates: abstracts by 11 June, acceptance notices by 30 June, final papers by 20 July, and the seminar on 31 July 2025.

How the team worked

  • Four sub-teams, each with named leads and defined responsibilities: organizing, content, moderation, and technical support.
  • Weekly coordination meetings among all teams and presenters.
  • A rehearsal before the event, and live technical support during the session.
  • A post-event survey designed around the CIPP model (context, input, process, product), so the results could be tied back to the seminar’s objectives.

What changed

The proposal planned 9 presentations in 3 hours. The event ran 4 presentations in about 90 minutes.

Results

The survey had 18 rating items on a 5-point scale. Average scores by CIPP area:

Area Mean (out of 5)
Context (needs and relevance) 4.263
Input (resources and planning) 4.200
Process (delivery and organization) 4.325
Product (learning outcomes) 4.367
Overall 4.289

Of 36 written comments, 22 praised the content, 10 asked for better time management and arrangement, and 4 asked for more Q&A time and hands-on examples.

What I would do differently

Give each presentation more time. Time and Q&A were the main points of feedback, so I would plan fewer, longer slots with a fixed block for questions.

Based on the Team B proposal and final report for ICT 401, Section 132, Rangsit University International College.
